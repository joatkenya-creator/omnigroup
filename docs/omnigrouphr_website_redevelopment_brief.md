# OmniGroup HR Website Review and Redevelopment Brief

**Reference website:** https://www.omnigrouphr.com/  
**Research date:** 24 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Source-grounded content and requirements brief for rebuilding OmniGroup HR in a new, empty repository.  
**Technology direction:** Next.js and TypeScript, deployed to Cloudflare. PHP and Laravel must not be used.

> **Important:** This document records information visible in the public website and publicly indexed pages during review. It is a redevelopment reference, not permission to copy the existing site's code, logo, or protected visual assets. Verify all business facts, service details, leadership information, claims, and contact information with the client before launch. Some linked pages were not fully accessible during this review, so missing details are marked for confirmation rather than invented.

---

## 1. Executive summary

OmniGroup HR presents itself as a talent, staffing, human-resources, workplace-support, and virtual-assistant services provider. Its public messaging emphasises connecting talent, empowering business success, operational efficiency, global hiring, and people-first support.

The homepage's headline positioning is:

- “CONNECTING TALENT”
- “EMPOWERING SUCCESS”
- “From VA services to full-scale workforce and HR solutions, we equip your business wit the talent and strategy it needs to thrive.” (The source page contains the typo “wit”; do not reproduce the typo in the new site.)
- “HR & Virtual Support That Powers Your Business”

The site should be redeveloped from scratch with a modern conversion-oriented experience. Preserve the company's existing business offering—including Virtual Assistant (VA) services—while improving clarity, service discovery, trust, accessibility, performance, and organic search visibility.

The current logo must **not** be reused. The new design should use a temporary typographic wordmark or neutral placeholder that is easy to replace when the approved logo becomes available. Do not invent a final logo.

## 4. Services to preserve and clarify

### 4.1 Virtual Assistant Services — mandatory to retain

The current site prominently promotes VA services. The About page describes executive assistants and virtual specialists who help streamline operations and scale efficiently. The public site also refers to operational and administrative support.

The new website must retain VA services as a clear, prominent offering—not bury it under HR or recruitment.

**Recommended page content structure:**

- Hero section explaining the business value of virtual support.
- Typical use cases and supported tasks, after the company confirms its actual scope.
- Types of VA support offered, such as executive or administrative support only where confirmed.
- Who the service is for.
- How the engagement and onboarding process works.
- How clients request or scope support.
- FAQs.
- A prominent “Discuss Your VA Needs” or “Request a Consultation” CTA.
- A short, accessible lead form.

**Details to confirm before publication:**

- Exact VA task categories.
- Supported working hours and time zones.
- Whether support is part-time, full-time, project-based, or all three.
- Pricing model and minimum engagement, if public.
- Candidate screening and onboarding process.
- Data security, confidentiality, and access procedures.
- Whether the company supplies individual VAs, teams, or managed services.
- Geographic availability and any industry-specific restrictions.

Do not invent these service details.

### 4.2 HR Services

The site says OmniGroup HR provides HR specialists, recruiters, and workforce strategists to help build teams, improve company culture, and increase operational efficiency.

Potential content areas to confirm with the company include HR consulting, HR operations, workforce planning, and talent acquisition. Publish only those that are actually offered.

### 4.3 Strategic Workforce Solutions / Staffing & Recruitment

The website describes recruitment, staffing, executive search, and workforce strategy. It highlights cross-border recruitment, cultural alignment, and helping organisations connect with talent worldwide.

Create a service page that explains the company's actual recruitment process, supported role types, client intake process, and engagement model once confirmed.

### 4.4 Mediation & Workplace Support

The homepage explicitly mentions Mediation & Workplace services and structured conflict resolution.

Build a dedicated page only after confirming the precise services, qualifications, jurisdictional limits, and intended clients. Avoid legal advice claims or guarantees about dispute outcomes.

### 4.5 Industry expertise

The About Us page lists these areas:

- Engineering: mechanical, electrical, and structural specialists.
- Oil & Gas: engineers, project managers, and technical experts.
- Infrastructure: civil engineers and project consultants for construction, transportation, and development.
- Finance: analysts, accountants, and financial strategists.
- HR Services: HR specialists, recruiters, and workforce strategists.

The site describes its work as connecting organisations with specialised talent and supporting complex hiring needs.

Validate the full industry list and each service's current availability with the client before building detailed landing pages.

## 7. Careers and recruitment

The site has Careers and Apply Now navigation links. The homepage encourages job seekers to contact OmniGroup HR about professional and non-professional opportunities.

The new website should support two clearly separated conversion paths:

1. **Employers:** Request talent, staffing, HR, or VA support.
2. **Candidates:** Explore genuine openings or submit an application through the approved recruitment process.

Do not create fake job listings, client names, salary ranges, or active openings. If no current jobs are available, show a clear candidate enquiry or talent-community option only if the business can process submissions.

Candidate forms must explain what information is collected and how it is used. CV uploads must be handled securely and not exposed through public URLs.

## 8. Newsletter

The existing website includes a newsletter sign-up with an email field, another input, a subscription checkbox, and a Submit button.

The actual behaviour and delivery destination were not verified during this review.

In the new site:

- Connect the form to an approved email marketing provider or backend.
- Use server-side validation.
- Include clear consent language and a privacy link.
- Show success only after the provider confirms successful subscription.
- Provide meaningful error and duplicate-subscription handling.
- Do not collect extra information without a defined need.

If no provider is configured, document the required configuration and do not simulate a successful submission.

## 9. Current website gaps and redevelopment opportunities

The following are implementation opportunities inferred from the public page structure, not a claim that every current feature is broken.

### Conversion opportunities

- Give employers a prominent primary CTA such as “Request a Consultation” or “Find Talent”.
- Give VA prospects a dedicated CTA and landing page.
- Give candidates a distinct Careers / View Opportunities route.
- Use short, service-specific forms rather than forcing every prospect into one generic contact form.
- Explain the next step after form submission.
- Add proof points only when verified: approved testimonials, case studies, client logos, outcomes, credentials, or service statistics.
- Use clear trust and process sections without inventing evidence.

### UX opportunities

- Simplify the navigation and make service categories easy to scan.
- Improve the hierarchy of the homepage.
- Make contact options accessible on mobile.
- Avoid repetitive generic paragraphs across pages.
- Add useful FAQs based on actual client questions.
- Ensure every CTA goes to a working page or functioning form.
- Design useful loading, validation, empty, and error states.

### SEO opportunities

- Give every indexable page a unique search intent and useful original content.
- Create dedicated, substantive service landing pages.
- Use descriptive titles, meta descriptions, canonical URLs, Open Graph metadata, and social-sharing images.
- Generate a sitemap and configure robots.txt appropriately.
- Add internal links between related services, industries, FAQs, and contact routes.
- Use semantic HTML and a logical heading hierarchy.
- Add appropriate structured data only where it reflects real business information.
- Optimise image sizes and formats and provide descriptive alt text.
- Avoid keyword stuffing, doorway pages, duplicated content, fabricated reviews, and unsupported “best” or “leading” claims.
- Research target keywords using current SEO tools and real search data before finalising page copy.
- Monitor indexing, conversions, and search performance after launch.

### Technical opportunities

- Build with Next.js and TypeScript.
- Deploy using a Cloudflare-compatible architecture.
- Prefer server rendering or static generation for SEO-critical pages where appropriate.
- Keep secrets in environment variables and server-side code.
- Add spam protection and server-side form validation.
- Include accessibility and performance testing.
- Keep the new codebase independent of the existing site's platform.

## 10. SEO and lead-generation plan

### Primary conversion types

1. Employer / client enquiry.
2. Virtual Assistant consultation.
3. Staffing and recruitment request.
4. HR services enquiry.
5. Mediation and workplace support enquiry.
6. Candidate application or careers enquiry.
7. Newsletter subscription, if it has a clear business purpose.

Track these as distinct conversion events where analytics consent and applicable privacy requirements permit.

### Suggested keyword research themes

These are seed topics for research, not confirmed search-volume opportunities:

- virtual assistant services for businesses
- executive virtual assistant services
- remote administrative support
- staffing and recruitment services
- executive search services
- HR consulting services
- workforce solutions
- engineering recruitment
- oil and gas recruitment
- infrastructure recruitment
- finance recruitment
- workplace mediation services

Prioritise the terms that match the company's actual service coverage and target market. Verify search demand, competition, geographic intent, and search-result intent before writing final metadata.

### Content strategy

Create useful, specific content that answers prospective clients' questions, for example:

- What tasks can a virtual assistant support?
- When should a business hire a virtual assistant versus an employee?
- How does the company's VA engagement process work?
- What should employers prepare before requesting staffing support?
- How does a recruitment engagement work?
- What should a business know before engaging workplace mediation services?

The company must review factual, operational, legal, HR, and compliance content before publication.

### Measurement

Set up an approved analytics solution and define conversion events for:

- Qualified enquiry form submission.
- VA consultation request.
- Recruitment / talent request.
- Click-to-call.
- Email click.
- Candidate application start and completion.
- Newsletter confirmation.

Do not treat page views or button clicks as equivalent to qualified leads. Use privacy-conscious measurement and verify that events actually fire.

## 12. Technology and hosting requirements

### Required

- Next.js with TypeScript.
- React and modern CSS / a maintainable styling system.
- Cloudflare-compatible hosting and deployment.
- No PHP.
- No Laravel.
- No dependency on the existing site's CMS or source repository.

### Cloudflare implementation

The coding agent must inspect the current Cloudflare-supported Next.js deployment options and choose a supported route for the project's needs. It must not assume that every Next.js feature runs identically on every Cloudflare product.

Use Cloudflare Pages or Workers with the currently supported Next.js deployment approach, selecting the architecture based on SSR, forms, image handling, and other runtime requirements.

Document the deployment process, environment variables, DNS/custom-domain steps, and any platform limitations. Never commit secrets.
